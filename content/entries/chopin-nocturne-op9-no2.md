Famous mostly for its melodic ornamentation — each return of the main
theme is more elaborately decorated than the last — but the passage
worth flagging for **mode mixture** comes late in the piece.

## The mixture moment

In the closing bars, the harmony borrows the flat submediant (C-flat
major, enharmonically respelled) from the parallel minor, coloring an
otherwise unclouded E-flat major close. It's a brief, controlled use
of mixture: one chord, not a modulation, functioning as a shadow that
passes before the final cadence.

## Why it's here

A good contrast case for mixture: compare against a piece where
mixture destabilizes the key area rather than just tinting a single
chord.
