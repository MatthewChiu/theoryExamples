The slow introduction is dense with applied chords, but the clearest
teaching example is the transition into the second theme group.

## The transition

The first theme sits firmly in C minor. The transition tonicizes E-flat
major (the relative major) by way of a **secondary dominant** — a V7/V
that is itself approached through a chain of applied chords rather
than a direct pivot. Students often confuse a secondary dominant with
a full modulation; this passage is useful precisely because it shows
both side by side: the applied chord resolves locally, and only a few
bars later does the music commit to E-flat as a new key area.

## Why it's here

Pair this with the Bach prelude's sequence for a discussion of how
Baroque sequential motion and Classical-era tonicization accomplish
different things with similar chromatic vocabulary.
