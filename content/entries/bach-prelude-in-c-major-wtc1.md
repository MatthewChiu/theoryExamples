The entire prelude is built from a single one-bar arpeggiated figure,
repeated harmony by harmony rather than developed melodically. What
holds the piece together is not a tune but a **harmonic sequence**: a
descending pattern of roots (I – ii7 – V6/5 of vi ... ) that carries
the ear forward measure by measure.

## Points worth noting

- The opening four bars sit entirely over a **tonic pedal** in the bass,
  even as the upper voices imply changing harmony above it — a good
  first example of pedal point for a student who has only seen it
  described abstractly.
- Around bar 19 the pattern locks into a descending-fifths sequence,
  the textbook version of the device: each chord is a fifth below (or
  a fourth above) the last.
- The close (bars 32–35) abandons the arpeggio pattern entirely and
  resolves over a dominant pedal, mirroring the tonic pedal of the
  opening.

## Why it's here

This is a useful "home base" example: almost every subsequent
sequence example in the index can be compared back to this one, since
it isolates the sequence from any competing melodic interest.
